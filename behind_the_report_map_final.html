Most Ready
Escambia

Northwest Florida's relationship with shipbuilding is generational. Escambia County sits at the center of a region where maritime work has shaped labor markets, skill pipelines, and community identity for decades. This cultural habituation matters. When interviewees described the Panhandle's workforce, they didn't talk about training programs or incentive packages first — they talked about people who already understood the work.

Importantly, Santa Rosa County directly next door, and nearby Bay and Calhoun counties around Panama City, also score high in readiness for revitalization — adding even more weight to the Northwest Florida Panhandle as a strong regional location for ship and module construction.

For Escambia and its NWF neighbors, investment isn't about creating a shipbuilding culture from scratch. It's about activating and revitalizing one that already exists.

Notably Ready
Brevard

Brevard County's profile is compelling based on quantitative statistics and historic track record relative to the high-tech world of the space race. The industrial infrastructure is there: deep-water port access, aerospace manufacturing capacity, skilled technical workforce, proximity to defense installations.

But the community-voiced, qualitative input adds important additional layers. Community members and workers identify strongly with aerospace. Community identity is focused on maintaining what residents see as an ideal balance among high-tech and high-wage space sector contributions, economic inputs from cruise ships and tourism, and strong preservation values for remaining coastal landscapes. Manufacturing valued in Brevard is largely consonant with High-Tech Corridor manufacturing, and less with the more traditional blue-collar manufacturing of shipbuilding.

Investments here in high-tech shipbuilding components will be well placed — still essential elements of a powerful, regionally distributed network of Florida maritime prosperity zones contributing to the nation's revitalized shipbuilding capacity.

Most Ready
Pinellas

In Pinellas, manufacturing and defense drive more economic activity than tourism. Raytheon, Honeywell, and a network of high-tech manufacturers anchor an industrial base that operates largely outside of daily public notice. The county produces microelectronics, medical devices, and defense components. The county's 2022 Target Employment and Industrial Land Study identified seven industrial clusters — from life sciences to aerospace — that signal robust participation in high-tech manufacturing.

Community interviews confirm what the quantitative data suggests: Pinellas is highly ready for successful participation in a revitalized shipbuilding economy. Since 2012, Pinellas has maintained a no-net-loss policy on industrial land — a policy almost unheard of in coastal Florida, where residential development pressure typically wins.

The county also solved a problem most regions struggle with: cross-jurisdictional coordination. Three bridges connect Pinellas, Hillsborough, and Pasco counties. Workers commute and goods transit fluidly across county lines — Pinellas, Hillsborough, and Pasco now function as a single labor market. Given its strength of social resilience, social capital, and business readiness, Pinellas's anchor tracts are strongly positioned for investment as the high-tech, manufacturing-centric anchor in the Tampa Bay area.
